# Regulatory Data Rules Shaping Richemont's Article/Material Master

*(RJC Chain-of-Custody excluded — already extracted separately)*

## 1. Kimberley Process Certification Scheme (KPCS)

Rough-diamond shipments require a validated **Kimberley Process (KP) Certificate** carrying, at minimum: country of origin (for unmixed-origin parcels) or country of provenance, a unique certificate number (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 country code prefix), issuing authority, dates of issuance/expiry, exporter and importer identification, carat weight, US-dollar value, number of parcels, and the Harmonized System code (7102.10/.21/.31) — per the [KP Core Document](https://www.worlddiamondcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/KP-Core-Document_20131122_amended.pdf). Importers must confirm receipt to the exporting authority with certificate number, parcel count, carat weight, and importer/exporter details, and keep the original certificate accessible for **no less than three years**; buyers/sellers/exporters are separately encouraged to keep **five-year** daily trading records (client names, license numbers, quantities, and values bought/sold/exported) in electronic, backed-up form ([KP Core Document](https://www.worlddiamondcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/KP-Core-Document_20131122_amended.pdf)).

## 2. EU Digital Product Passport (DPP) under ESPR

The Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 establishes the DPP framework, with per-category data fields set by later delegated acts rather than the base regulation itself ([spilma DPP guide](https://www.spilma.com/en/guides/digital-product-passport-dpp)). The Commission's first ESPR Working Plan 2025–2030, adopted 16 April 2025, places **textiles/apparel** in the first priority wave with an indicative 2027 delegated-act adoption date; footwear is excluded from wave one pending a study due end-2027, and jewellery/watches remain outside the confirmed plan, effectively "under review" ([European Commission ESPR FAQ](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f7ff5e2-ebe9-4bd4-a139-db881bd6398f_en?filename=FAQ-UPDATE-4th-Iteration_clean.pdf); [Sidley Austin](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/04/european-commission-releases-list-of-priority-products)). Recurring DPP data categories across the framework include: unique product/operator identifier and batch/serial, material composition and critical-raw-material content, presence of substances of concern, recycled-content share, durability/reliability data, repairability indicators and spare-part availability, disassembly/end-of-life routing instructions, and conformity-declaration references, accessible via a QR code, data matrix, or RFID carrier ([spilma DPP guide](https://www.spilma.com/en/guides/digital-product-passport-dpp)). Separately, from 19 July 2026 large enterprises face a ban on destroying unsold apparel, clothing accessories, and footwear (HS 4203, 61, 62, 6401–6405), with disclosure obligations on discarded volumes ([Green Forum, European Commission](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/implementing-ecodesign-sustainable-products-regulation_en)).

## 3. Swiss Made Ordinance for Watches ("60% rule")

Under the revised Ordinance on the Use of "Swiss" for Watches (in force 1 January 2017), **at least 60% of the manufacturing cost of the complete watch** as an end product must be generated in Switzerland; for the movement specifically, at least **half of its component value** must be Swiss-made and at least **60% of movement manufacturing cost** must be Swiss-generated, with technical development of both watch and movement required to occur in Switzerland ([Swiss Federal Institute of Intellectual Property, IGE](https://www.ige.ch/en/law-and-policy/national-ip-law/indications-of-source/swiss-indications-of-source/industry-ordinances/revision-of-the-ordinance-on-the-use-of-swiss-for-watches)). This requires a bill-of-materials cost breakdown by component origin (Swiss vs. non-Swiss manufacturing cost and component value), supporting invoices, and R&D-location records to substantiate the 60% and 50%-value thresholds; casings and glass had a transitional exclusion from the cost calculation only until 31 December 2018 for pre-existing stock ([IGE](https://www.ige.ch/en/law-and-policy/national-ip-law/indications-of-source/swiss-indications-of-source/industry-ordinances/revision-of-the-ordinance-on-the-use-of-swiss-for-watches)). "Swiss" is a legally protected geographical indication under WTO TRIPS Article 22 ([IGE](https://www.ige.ch/en/law-and-policy/national-ip-law/indications-of-source/swiss-indications-of-source/industry-ordinances/revision-of-the-ordinance-on-the-use-of-swiss-for-watches)).

## 4. Hallmarking regimes

**UK (Hallmarking Act 1973, four Assay Offices):** a full UK hallmark requires the sponsor's/maker's mark (≥2 unique letters identifying who submitted the item), a metal-fineness mark in parts-per-thousand, and the Assay Office town mark; an optional date letter records the year ([UK gov.uk hallmarking guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/hallmarking-guidance-notes/hallmarking-is-the-law-guidance-summary)). **France (Poinçon de maître/de responsabilité, Direction Générale des Douanes):** professionals must file a déclaration d'existence with the bureau de garantie, keep a *livre de police* (police-book transaction ledger), insculpate their maker's mark (fabricants) or responsibility mark (importers) on a copper plate held by the bureau de garantie, and have items additionally struck with the state guarantee mark (poinçon de garantie) before sale ([Douanes françaises](http://www.douane.gouv.fr/demarche/marquer-vos-ouvrages-dun-poincon-de-maitre-ou-de-responsabilite)). **Switzerland (Bureau central du contrôle des métaux précieux):** manufacturers/importers of watch cases and precious-metal articles are assigned a registered responsibility mark and fineness/control mark under the precious-metals control legislation, with per-item control-office recording analogous to the French/UK systems (national precious-metals-control framework).

## 5. German Supply Chain Act (LkSG) and CS3D

The LkSG requires in-scope enterprises (≥1,000 employees in Germany since 2024) to run a human-rights/environmental risk-management system covering their own operations, direct contractual partners, and — on substantiated knowledge — indirect suppliers, with mandatory complaints procedures and regular reporting ([csr-in-deutschland.de, German Federal Government](https://www.csr-in-deutschland.de/EN/Legislation/German-Supply-Chain-Act/supply-chain-act-art.html)). The EU's Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CS3D/CSDDD, Directive (EU) 2024/1760) extends this EU-wide to companies above 1,000 employees/€450m turnover (phasing in 2027–2029 by size), requiring due-diligence integration, impact identification/assessment, prevention/mitigation plans, contractual safeguards with business partners, notification/complaints mechanisms, annual public statements, and climate transition plans across upstream and limited downstream "chains of activities" ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/03/human-rights-and-the-environment--what-to-expect-from-the-corporate-sustainability-due-diligence-directive); [EUR-Lex Directive 2024/1760](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/1760/oj/eng)). Companies must **retain due-diligence documentation for at least 5 years** from creation, extended if litigation is pending ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/1760/oj/eng)).

## 6. CITES for exotic leathers (alligator, python, coral)

Most alligator/crocodile and python species used in luxury leather goods and watch straps sit under **CITES Appendix II**, permitting trade only with valid export permits (and import permits where required by the importing state); Appendix I species are excluded from commercial straps entirely ([CITES Convention text](https://cites.org/eng/disc/text.php); [Chroonoo CITES guide](https://www.chroonoo.com/guides/cites-certification-for-watch-straps-exotic-leather-sourcing-regulations-explained/)). Export of an Appendix I specimen requires prior grant and presentation of an export permit, issued only after the exporting state's Scientific Authority confirms the export is not detrimental to species survival; re-export requires an equivalent re-export certificate ([CITES Article IV/V, cites.org](https://cites.org/eng/disc/text.php)). Each qualifying skin/strap ships with a tag encoding the issuing country, permit/CITES number, position number on the permit, and species name, forming the per-shipment chain-of-custody record referenced in industry guidance on exotic watch-strap sourcing, a documented practice for luxury houses including Cartier ([Chroonoo CITES guide](https://www.chroonoo.com/guides/cites-certification-for-watch-straps-exotic-leather-sourcing-regulations-explained/)).

## 7. GDPR, PIPL, and CCPA on cross-market client-master data

Under GDPR, personal data may only leave the EU/UK via an adequacy decision, or safeguards such as Standard Contractual Clauses or Binding Corporate Rules, each requiring a documented Transfer Impact/Risk Assessment; the US is not automatically adequate outside the EU-US Data Privacy Framework ([Finnegan LLP](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/luxury-brands-global-data-and-personalized-marketing-tips-for-compliance-in-the-uk-eu-and-us.html); [GDPR Chapter 5](https://gdpr-info.eu/chapter-5/)). China's PIPL requires a Data Transfer Mechanism — security assessment, CAC standard contractual clauses, or certification — for cross-border personal-information export, with security assessment mandatory for "important data" or critical-infrastructure operators regardless of volume-based exemptions, and separate consent generally required unless a contractual/HR/vital-interest legal basis applies ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/admin/upload/SiteAttachments/china-finalises-exemptions-to-cross-border-data-transfer-rules-and-eases-restrictions.pdf)). California's CCPA/CPRA gives consumers rights to know, delete, and limit use of personal information collected by covered businesses, obligating retention-period disclosure and deletion capability for client-master records. Across Richemont's 45 ELEVATE markets, this layers jurisdiction-specific consent flags, transfer-mechanism metadata, and retention/deletion triggers onto every client record touched by clienteling, CRM, and after-sales systems.

## SAP Implications

- **Material master classification**: DPP-relevant fields (material composition, recycled content, repairability score) and Swiss Made BOM cost splits need dedicated classification views/characteristics on the material master, likely via classification (CT04/CL02) or custom Z-fields tied to a compliance-relevant material type.
- **Batch-managed materials**: KP-certified rough-diamond lots and CITES-tagged exotic-leather components should be handled as batch-managed materials so certificate/permit numbers, origin, and weight/value travel with the batch through goods movements and are traceable at sales-order and delivery level.
- **EHS / Product Compliance module (SAP EHSM / Product Compliance for Discrete Industries)**: is the natural home for substances-of-concern tracking, DPP data-carrier generation, and Swiss-Made/hallmarking compliance-declaration references, since it already models regulatory listings and declarable substances.
- **SAP GTS (Global Trade Services) for CITES**: export/import permit numbers, non-detriment findings, and species-level HS classification fit GTS's legal-control and license-determination functionality, enabling automated compliance checks at order and shipment creation.
- **MDG-BP (Master Data Governance – Business Partner) for KYC/counterparties**: KP-scheme exporters/importers, CITES-permitted suppliers, and LkSG/CS3D tier-1–N suppliers all require governed business-partner records with risk-assessment attributes, consent/transfer-mechanism flags (GDPR/PIPL/CCPA), and audit trails — a natural extension of MDG-BP's central governance workflow.
