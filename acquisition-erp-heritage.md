# Richemont Acquisition Stream and ERP Heritage

## Narrative

Richemont's Maison portfolio was not built as a single company adopting one system from day one — it was assembled through roughly forty years of overlapping holding-company transactions, and the IT record follows that same messy lineage. The earliest strand runs through **Cartier Monde**, formed in 1979 to unite Cartier's Paris, London and New York operations, which acquired **Piaget** and **Baume & Mercier** (60%, rising to full ownership by 1993) in April 1988 ([UPI](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1988/04/26/Cartier-acquires-control-of-Baume-et-Mercier-Piaget/8399578030400/), [Watch Wiki](https://www.watch-wiki.net/doku.php?id=vendome_luxury_group)). In parallel, **Alfred Dunhill Ltd.** had held Montblanc since 1977 (full ownership 1985) and acquired **Chloé** in 1985 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlo%C3%A9), [Alfred Dunhill Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Dunhill_Limited)). These two chains merged in 1993, when Dunhill Holding (57% owned by Rothmans) combined with Cartier to form **Groupe Vendôme / Vendôme Luxury Group**, controlled by the newly created Compagnie Financière Richemont (1988) ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/vendome-luxury-group-plc), [Alfred Dunhill Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Dunhill_Limited)). Vendôme then bought **Vacheron Constantin** in 1996 and **Panerai** and **Lancel** in 1997 ([Richemont history](https://www.richemont.com/about-us/history/), [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/1996/12/13/business/vendome-buys-a-watchmaker.html)), before Richemont took full ownership of Vendôme's luxury interests in 1998 ([Richemont history](https://www.richemont.com/about-us/history/)).

None of these pre-1998 deals come with public disclosure of the target's back-office or ERP systems — 1980s-1990s watch and leather-goods houses of this size typically ran on paper, mainframe, or bespoke local systems, and none of the primary transaction announcements or later corporate histories mention them. The public record only starts describing systems once Richemont began building its own group template. The template — internally called **"Gemini"** (originally rendered SAP GEMINI, succeeding an earlier "SAP Tiger" instance for some Maisons) — traces to a SAP program that started with the creation of a Geneva SAP competence centre and rolled out first to core Watch & Jewellery Maisons ([LinkedIn: Philippe Leclercq](https://www.linkedin.com/in/philippe-leclercq-b2279419a); [LinkedIn: Roch Lacaille](https://www.linkedin.com/in/roch-lacaille-0a17106b)). Cartier, Piaget, IWC, Jaeger-LeCoultre, Van Cleef & Arpels, Montblanc, and Vacheron Constantin all show long-tenured staff in Gemini/SAP key-user, template and rollout roles going back to the 2007–2014 window ([LinkedIn: Edouard Guibert](https://www.linkedin.com/in/edouard-guibert-0905), [LinkedIn: Paul Lai](https://www.linkedin.com/in/paullai1), [LinkedIn: Victor Chan](https://www.linkedin.com/in/vcvictorchan)), confirming Gemini as Richemont's long-standing common ERP template rather than a recent initiative.

The 2000 **LMH (Les Manufactures Horlogères)** acquisition brought IWC, Jaeger-LeCoultre and A. Lange & Söhne in as a package with no public detail on their pre-acquisition systems, but LinkedIn profiles confirm both IWC and JLC now run SAP/Gemini with long-standing SAP key-user roles ([LinkedIn: Frank Lee](https://www.linkedin.com/in/frank-lee-31980123)). Later, more clearly documented acquisitions show the group's actual playbook: Roger Dubuis (60% stake, 2008) runs SAP Sales & Distribution under Richemont's Business Process Owner structure ([LinkedIn: Cogordan-Pin](https://www.linkedin.com/in/cogordan-pin)); Watchfinder (2018) had its own UK finance system before Richemont's finance team explicitly implemented SAP post-acquisition ([LinkedIn: Julie Langley](https://www.linkedin.com/in/julielangley)); Buccellati (2019) shows a dedicated internal SAP project-manager and analyst headcount from 2021 onward, and Richemont's FY22 annual report confirms Buccellati's Latin America integration and system go-live occurred in Q1 FY22 ([Richemont FY22 Business Review](https://www.richemont.com/media/nqqhh5dc/richemont-fy22-annual-report-business-review-en.pdf)); and Delvaux's Head of IT explicitly describes leading "the full integration of the Information Systems into the Group ecosystem" after the June 2021 acquisition, including migration between PLM, SAP AFS and SAP ECC ([LinkedIn: Annaïck S.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/anna%C3%AFcksolleu), [LinkedIn: Cédric Dejalle](https://www.linkedin.com/in/cedricdejalle)). Vhernier, completed 12 September 2024 for €94 million net cash consideration ([Richemont FY25 Interim Results](https://www.richemont.com/media/5hibkj00/ad-hoc-announcement-pursuant-to-art-53-lr-fy25-interim-results-en.pdf)), retains its own IT Director role even as an "IT Director Vhernier – Richemont Group" ([LinkedIn: Silvia Rovati](https://www.linkedin.com/in/silvia-rovati-629845170)), which is consistent with an integration still in progress rather than completed.

The clearest and largest non-SAP tail system in Richemont's history is **Net-a-Porter / YNAP**. Built on proprietary Yoox and Net-a-Porter e-commerce technology pre-merger, YNAP explicitly replaced this with IBM WebSphere Commerce and IBM Order Management in 2016 as a "single IT platform" following the 2015 Yoox merger ([Computing.co.uk](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2449929/yoox-net-a-porter-group-picks-ibm-for-game-changing-post-merger-tech-platform-confirms-cio), [FashionNetwork](https://ww.fashionnetwork.com/news/Online-retailer-yoox-net-a-porter-adopts-ibm-software,666128.html)), later layering in Centric Software PLM (2017) and RELEX supply-chain planning ([Centric Software](https://www.centricsoftware.com/success-stories/yoox-net-a-porter/), [RELEX Solutions](https://www.relexsolutions.com/resources/ynap/)) — while some finance staff simultaneously carried SAP ERP and Dynamics 365 Business Central roles ([LinkedIn: Waris Syed](https://www.linkedin.com/in/waris-syed-962957249)). This entire multi-system stack exited the group when Mytheresa completed its YNAP acquisition on 23 April 2025 ([Richemont press releases](https://www.richemont.com/news-media/press-releases-news/richemont-acquires-vhernier/)), so it is now a former rather than current tail risk, but it demonstrates that even a 23-year-owned digital business never fully converged onto Gemini.

## The acquisition-heritage risk in one paragraph

Forty years of holding-company deals — Cartier Monde, Vendôme, LMH, and two decades of one-off Maison purchases — mean Richemont's "single" Gemini/SAP template is really a palimpsest: each Maison joined at a different point in the template's own evolution (some still show traces of a predecessor "SAP Tiger" instance being interfaced into Gemini as recently as 2024, per one Richemont accountant's LinkedIn profile), so company codes, chart-of-accounts structures, and article/customer master data reflect whatever localization each Maison's entity needed at its go-live date rather than a harmonized design. Newly acquired Maisons (Vhernier, Gianvito Rossi) still run their own IT organizations with no public confirmation of Gemini migration, meaning fresh legacy master data and local financial systems are still being folded in even as older tail systems linger — YNAP ran IBM/Centric/RELEX for its entire ownership life and never touched Gemini before divestment, and Watchfinder's AWS-native platform required a dedicated post-acquisition SAP build-out rather than inheriting one. For a Bluefield S/4HANA conversion, this heritage is the central risk: any selective data-transition exercise must contend with multiple generations of chart-of-accounts design decisions baked into decades-old company codes, master data that was never re-harmonized across the Cartier-Monde-era Maisons versus the SAP-native ones, and a population of recently acquired or historically carve-out Maisons (Vhernier, Gianvito Rossi, the former YNAP estate) whose systems of record may not cleanly map onto the group template being carried forward — meaning the conversion scope decision (what to bring across versus rebuild) needs Maison-by-Maison technical due diligence, not a single group-wide assumption.
