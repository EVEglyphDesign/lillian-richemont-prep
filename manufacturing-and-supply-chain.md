# Richemont: Manufacturing and Supply-Chain Integration Depth

## 1. Manufacture status by Maison

Richemont's watch Maisons sit on a spectrum from full vertical integration ("Manufacture" status — designing, producing and assembling movements in-house) to reliance on third-party ébauche suppliers such as ETA (Swatch Group) and Sellita.

**Jaeger-LeCoultre** is one of the most vertically integrated watchmakers in Switzerland: its Vallée de Joux site brings together 82 workshops and produces over 1,200 in-house movement variants under one roof, covering design through final assembly ([Jaeger-LeCoultre](https://www.jaeger-lecoultre.com/us-en/our-maison/manufacture-since-1833)), a scope corroborated by trade coverage noting more than 1,200 staff and full in-house component production ([Buro 24/7](https://buro247.me/jewellery-and-watches/buro-loves-jewellery-and-watches/inside-jaeger-lecoultre-s-manufacture-where-clocks/)).

**Vacheron Constantin** produces all of its in-house movements at its Plan-les-Ouates and Le Brassus sites, including hairspring production, gem-setting, engraving, guillochage and enamelling; notably, cases and most dials are *not* made in-house and are sourced externally ([WatchesBySJX](https://watchesbysjx.com/2024/07/vacheron-constantin-manufacture-visit.html)). The Maison consolidated component manufacturing into a single new Le Brassus facility ([WorldTempus](https://en.worldtempus.com/article/industry-news/brands/vacheron-constantin-new-component-manufacturing-facility-15917.html)), though historically it used JLC-supplied ébauches before achieving full autonomy ([Quill & Pad](https://quillandpad.com/2016/06/09/history-vacheron-constantins-overseas-line-culminating-2016s-worldtimer/)).

**A. Lange & Söhne** states every watch bearing its name uses an in-house-produced "manufacture movement," with more than 70 calibres developed since its 1990 relaunch in Glashütte ([A. Lange & Söhne](https://www.alange-soehne.com/us-en/manufacture/art-of-watchmaking/manufacture-movements)), each finished and hand-assembled twice on site ([A. Lange & Söhne](https://www.alange-soehne.com/us-en/manufacture)).

**IWC Schaffhausen** is a partial case: it produced its own movements from 1868 until the 1970s quartz crisis, then relied on outsourced base movements (including ETA, later Sellita for its 7750-based chronograph clones) with in-house modules added, before returning to full in-house design with calibre 5000 in 2000 and its first in-house chronograph (calibre 89360) in 2007 ([IWC](https://www.iwc.com/ch-en/journal/mechanical-marvels-part1); [Watch Wiki](https://www.watch-wiki.net/doku.php?id=IWC_calibres)). Entry-level IWC lines still use externally sourced high-quality ETA movements alongside four in-house calibre families ([Watchuseek](https://www.watchuseek.com/threads/iwc-eta.651772/)).

**Roger Dubuis** is described as a Manufacture that produces "most of the components of its movements in-house" and is the only Maison with production nearly 100% certified under the Poinçon de Genève quality hallmark ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Dubuis); [Roger Dubuis](https://www.rogerdubuis.com/our-maison/craftsmanship/poincon-de-geneve)).

**Piaget** is one of the few Maisons that both designs and manufactures its own movements, having pioneered the ultra-thin category (2mm hand-wound calibre 9P, 1957; 2.3mm automatic calibre 12P, 1960) and historically supplied ébauches/components to other watchmakers ([Piaget](https://www.piaget.com/us-en/movement); [WatchProSite](https://www.watchprosite.com/piaget/piaget--a-rightful-manufacture--part-1-geneva/881.406781.2430421/)). Its Group-owned refinery also supplies 100% of its fine gold ([Swisswatches Magazine](https://swisswatches-magazine.com/piaget-manufacture-visit-a-true-maison/)).

**Baume & Mercier** relied largely on externally sourced ETA/Sellita base calibres (e.g., ETA 2824-2-based BM 112824, Sellita SW200-1-based Classima models) rather than in-house movements ([CaliberCorner](https://calibercorner.com/baume-mercier-caliber-bm-112824/); [WatchBase](https://watchbase.com/baume-mercier/classima/8689); [The Watch Company](https://www.thewatchcompany.com/blog/the-sellita-sw200-guide/)). Richemont completed the sale of 100% of Baume & Mercier to Italy's Damiani Group on 1 July 2026, with Richemont providing transitional operational services during the handover ([Richemont](https://www.richemont.com/news-media/press-releases-news/richemont-and-the-damiani-group-announce-the-completion-of-the-acquisition-of-baume-mercier-by-the-damiani-group/)).

## 2. Richemont's in-house component operations

Richemont has built a layer of Group-owned component manufacturers that supply multiple Maisons, distinct from Maison-specific manufactures:

- **Manufacture Horlogère ValFleurier** — a Richemont-owned movement manufacturer spread across Neuchâtel (R&D), Buttes (production/machining) and La Côte-aux-Fées (finishing/decoration), designing 5–6 new calibres a year and producing/finishing over 20,000 movements, supplying Montblanc, Panerai, Baume & Mercier and, to a lesser extent, Piaget and IWC ([Europa Star](https://www.europastar.com/magazine/highlights/1002198110-val-fleurier-responds-to-richemont-s-movement.html); [Grail Watch Wiki](https://wiki.grail-watch.com/index.php/ValFleurier)).
- **Donzé-Baume SA** — acquired by Richemont in November 2007; a Jura-based watch-case and bracelet manufacturer (c. 300 employees) that already supplied several Swiss watchmakers, including Richemont Maisons, before the deal ([Richemont](https://www.richemont.com/news-media/press-releases-news/richemont-acquires-watch-component-manufacturer-done-baume-sa/)).
- **Varinor / Varin-Étampage (VVSA)** — acquired by Richemont in October 2012; a Delémont-based precious-metals refiner and stamped-exterior-component maker for watches and jewellery, also Responsible Jewellery Council Chain-of-Custody certified for gold and platinum refining ([Richemont](https://www.richemont.com/news-media/press-releases-news/richemont-acquires-varin-etampage-and-varinor-vvsa/); [RJC certificate](https://www.responsiblejewellery.com/wp-content/uploads/Varinor-SA-RJC_Certificate-COC-2017-EU-v2.pdf)).
- **Roger Dubuis component-manufacturing arm** — Richemont acquired the standalone component-production facility of Manufacture Roger Dubuis SA in September 2007, ahead of taking a 60% equity stake in the brand itself the following year ([Richemont, 2007](https://www.richemont.com/news-media/press-releases-news/richemont-acquires-component-manufacturing-operations-of-manufacturer-roger-dubuis-sa/); [Richemont, 2008](https://www.richemont.com/news-media/press-releases-news/richemont-acquires-60-per-cent-interest-in-manufacturer-roger-dubuis-sa/)).
- **Cartier — Manufacture La Chaux-de-Fonds** — a 33,000 m² site (completed 2000) that consolidated previously scattered production; on average a third of any given component (hands, dials, screws) is made in-house, with the remainder outsourced — for example, roughly two-thirds of hands come from external suppliers Universo and Aiguilla ([WatchProZine](https://www.watchprozine.com/cartier/live-from-la-chaux-de-fonds-the-cartier-manufacture/3838926/886/); [WatchProSite](https://www.watchprosite.com/digest/cartier-manufacture-la-chaux-de-fonds-tour/)). The site includes a dedicated enamelling workshop and high-jewellery workshop, and its *Maison des Métiers d'Art* (opened 2014) gathers specialist watchmaking crafts ([FHS](https://www.fhs.swiss/eng/2024_10_17_01_Cartier.html)).

## 3. Jewellery-side atelier operations

Cartier consolidated its various French production sites into a single Paris atelier in the 9th arrondissement in September 2016, described as the largest fine-jewellery workshop in the world, employing close to 200 skilled craftspeople under one roof ([Fashion Network](https://ww.fashionnetwork.com/news/Cartier-unveils-its-high-jewellery-workshop-the-largest-in-the-world,1588081.html)). Van Cleef & Arpels maintains its atelier and haute-joaillerie savoir-faire around Place Vendôme, and supports L'ÉCOLE, School of Jewelry Arts, which since 2024 has an expanded Paris campus at the Hôtel de Mercy-Argenteau ([L'ÉCOLE](https://www.lecolevancleefarpels.com/fr/en); [Fashionista](https://fashionista.com/2017/10/van-cleef-arpels-paris-school-lecole)). Place Vendôme itself remains the historic hub where Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels and rival Maisons have run boutiques and workshops side-by-side since the early 1900s ([South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/article/732986/got-it-made)). As with watch components, in-house ateliers coexist with a subcontractor network for casings, findings and stone-setting on lower-tier lines, mirroring the broader jewellery industry's reliance on external casting houses and setters described below.

## 4. Typical outsourcing pattern (Swiss watch/jewellery industry vs Richemont-specific)

Outsourcing dials, hands, straps, cases and stone-setting to specialist subcontractors is the industry norm, not a Richemont anomaly. Independent suppliers such as H-Development custom-produce dials, hands, cases, buckles and bracelets for multiple brands ([H-Development](https://h-development.ch/en/watch-components/)); Bryek Horlogerie/Magstein specialize in assembly and mechanical-movement services for numerous watchmakers ([Bryek](https://bryek.com/)); ABP Genève relies on a network of trusted dial makers, case makers, engravers, enamellers and gem setters ([ABP Genève](https://www.abpconcept.paris/pages/geneve)); and firms like Oesch supply buckles and metal fittings across the strap trade ([Kompass](https://lb.kompass.com/z/ch/a/watch-bracelets-and-straps/45500/)). In jewellery, casting houses (e.g., Weston Beamor, Lukas Casting House) offer end-to-end outsourced casting, setting and polishing services used broadly across the trade ([Weston Beamor](https://www.westonbeamor.com/about-us/who-we-work-with); [Lukas Casting House](https://jewelrylukas.com/outsourcing-jewelry-production/)). What is comparatively Richemont-specific is the Group's strategy of *acquiring* select component suppliers outright (Donzé-Baume, Varinor, Roger Dubuis's component arm, ValFleurier) rather than only contracting with independents — internalizing a slice of the subcontractor base while still leaving straps, most dials, gem-cutting and casting largely in the external network.

## 5. Distribution model

Richemont operates a boutique-led, largely directly-controlled distribution network: as of March 2025, the Group reported 2,463 total monobrand boutiques (1,392 internal/directly-operated plus 1,071 franchised) ([Richemont FY25 results](https://www.richemont.com/media/nptj0zrk/richemont-fy25-annual-results-presentation-en.pdf)); a more recent Group presentation shows 2,376 total boutiques as of March 2026 (1,393 internal) ([Richemont Group presentation](https://www.richemont.com/media/dtjamuw0/richemont-group-presentation-en.pdf)), with retail representing roughly two-thirds of Group sales in FY2024/25 versus under 50% five years prior ([Porter's Five Force analysis](https://portersfiveforce.com/blogs/marketing-strategy/richemont)). **Watchfinder & Co.**, acquired by Richemont in 2018 for its c.200 employees and seven boutiques ([Richemont](https://www.richemont.com/news-media/press-releases-news/richemont-acquires-watchfindercouk-limited/)), operates as the Group's pre-owned lane, buying, servicing and reselling watches from over 60 brands and, since around 2022–23, adding a third-party marketplace model alongside its own-inventory business ([Robb Report](https://robbreport.com/style/watch-collector/watchfinder-marketplace-1234779521/); [Richemont](https://www.richemont.com/our-maisons/watchfinder-co/)). **ELEVATE**, launched in October 2019, is Richemont's Group-wide clienteling and CRM platform built on Salesforce and Google Cloud, unifying client data across Maisons for 360-degree client views, marketing automation and in-store clienteling apps; it now spans 11+ brands across 45 markets and roughly 10,000–11,000 users ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/eu/blog/richemont-maisons-delight-customers/); [LinkedIn/Philippe Meyer](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/richemont-hires-key-roles-boost-client-centricity-elevate-meyer); [Undisturbed](https://undisturbed.blog/articles/richemont)).

## Data implications for SAP

- **Material master granularity**: In-house-manufactured movement components (JLC, Vacheron, Lange, Roger Dubuis, Piaget) need full BOM/routing data in the material master, while ETA/Sellita-sourced calibres (historically Baume & Mercier, entry IWC lines) should be modeled as simple procured trading-good materials — the two groups cannot share one generic "movement" material type without losing this distinction.
- **Subcontracting scenario (SAP outsource manufacturing, scenario 30)**: Group-owned but legally separate component makers (ValFleurier, Donzé-Baume, Varinor, the Roger Dubuis component arm) are strong candidates for classic subcontracting POs — Richemont provides the components/precious metal as consigned components, the subcontractor performs the operation, and goods receipt consumes the components — rather than treating them as arm's-length vendors on standard PO flows, given the SAP case study confirms "outsource manufacturing" was explicitly one of the four level-one processes piloted with the six Maisons ([SAP news](https://news.sap.com/africa/2023/05/richemont-harmonizing-supply-chain-processes-between-group-business-and-suppliers/)).
- **Consignment stock at ateliers**: Precious-metal and gemstone flows into Cartier's Paris high-jewellery atelier and Cartier/Vacheron's Swiss manufactures likely warrant consignment or special-stock-indicator handling (vendor- or plant-consigned), since raw gold/platinum from Varinor and gemstones entering Group ateliers carry high unit value and traceability/RJC chain-of-custody requirements that standard unrestricted-use stock does not capture.
- **Supplier-tiering in Business Network**: Only 17 of ~460 direct suppliers on SAP Business Network have their own-ERP integration back to Richemont ([SAP news](https://news.sap.com/africa/2023/05/richemont-harmonizing-supply-chain-processes-between-group-business-and-suppliers/)); the acquired/Group-owned component makers (ValFleurier, Donzé-Baume, Varinor) are the most plausible candidates to prioritize for that tier, since they are captive, high-volume, and structurally close to Richemont — unlike the long tail of independent dial/strap/case subcontractors (H-Development, Bryek, ABP Genève-type suppliers) who more plausibly sit in the portal-only, non-integrated segment.
- **Vendor master vs. intercompany**: Because several "suppliers" (ValFleurier, Donzé-Baume, Varinor, Roger Dubuis's component arm) are wholly or majority Richemont-owned, transactions with them may need intercompany billing/STO logic rather than standard third-party vendor invoicing — worth confirming against the legal-entity structure behind the 700+ direct-supplier count.
- **Divestiture data cleanup**: The Baume & Mercier sale to Damiani (completed 1 July 2026, with Richemont providing interim operational services) ([Richemont](https://www.richemont.com/news-media/press-releases-news/richemont-and-the-damiani-group-announce-the-completion-of-the-acquisition-of-baume-mercier-by-the-damiani-group/)) implies a live carve-out of that Maison's vendor master records, ValFleurier movement-supply agreements, and SAP Business Network relationships out of Richemont's system landscape — a near-term data-migration event distinct from steady-state supplier integration work.
- **Boutique consignment vs. sale**: With 1,392+ internally-operated boutiques versus 1,071 franchised units ([Richemont FY25 results](https://www.richemont.com/media/nptj0zrk/richemont-fy25-annual-results-presentation-en.pdf)), inventory-ownership rules differ by boutique type (owned stock in internal boutiques vs. consignment/sale-or-return terms likely governing franchise and some wholesale/Watchfinder marketplace inventory), which affects where "consignment stock" logic should be scoped beyond just manufacturing ateliers.
