New Foundations — a sovereign PMO reading

Shift from vendor-led discovery to client-led. A framing walk-through, not a proposal.

These are the four pages sketched by hand on 16 August 2026, rewritten as a single read so we can walk them together. Nothing here is a decision. It is the framing I want to be sure we share before we talk about a role, a team, or a plan.

The programme, as it is being staffed

Richemont is currently hiring three New Foundations roles into Meyrin (Geneva), hybrid, full-time — each posting reports well over one hundred applicants in the first day:

  • New Foundations — S/4HANA Transformation Technical Manager — the technical spine of the future-state ERP.
  • New Foundations — Scope & Simplification Manager — the discipline that decides what is in and what is out, and stops the additive drift.
  • New Foundations — Programme Manager — the delivery authority that holds the three together.

Read as a ring, these three roles are the operating shape of a sovereign PMO in everything but the word: a technical authority for the ledger spine, a scope authority for the estate, and a programme authority above both. The recap below is written to be legible against that ring.

1The estate — sovereign vs rented

Any transformation programme is inherited on two sides at once. On one side is the estate the company already runs; on the other is the estate the company could own. The first job is to be able to point at a system and say honestly which side it sits on.

Sovereign — new world owned

  • A PMO tool built on an advanced, data-driven assessment, run by an agent that lives in the client's own DMZ and reaches into the hyperscaler the client already pays for.
  • ACDOCA as the ledger spine, read through a datasphere semantic layer the client controls — so the same numbers a vendor would show back live under a client authority.
  • An extended ledger / data lake that the sovereign agent structure both reads and writes.
  • Outputs land in a Decision Intelligence Center of Excellence, backed by a quantified business case and eDiscovery tooling.

Rented — old world inherited

  • A single S/4 future state on paper, sitting on top of many ECCs in practice.
  • PTP fractured across many brands and many SAPs.
  • Focus is additive — new modules, new licences — and the technical leakage compounds underneath.

Between the two sides is a semantic mirror. Same ledger, different authority. The reason to draw the picture is not to reject the rented estate — it is to make the sovereign estate nameable, so a domain lead can say this system is sovereign, that one is rented without the room dissolving into vendor grammar.

What the CIO takeaway actually is

A transformation programme is judged by two things, not by the count of go-lives: OpEx efficiency booked inside SAP, and CapEx strategy that appreciates a sovereign domain the client owns. The PMO's business cases have to be quantified from source data, not from vendor slides. That is the capital-asset lens.

2Why the familiar shape lands at fifty percent

There is a familiar shape in large programmes: Ariba stitched to S/4, no-PO-no-pay as the control, invoice-to-cash as the value chain, a big SI holding the P2P and R2R actualisation. On paper the architecture is correct. In the room, the case lives or dies on whether that partnership actually closes the last mile.

The honest number, when it does not, is roughly fifty percent. Half the invoices do not land against the control the business case was sold on. The software is fine. The partnership model is where the case leaks out.

You are still describing the steps to do, rather than the expected outcome — why and how is it better or different from what they might be doing already. — note left on the page

That note is the right redirect, and it applies here as much as it applied to the drawing. A CIO sponsor does not buy an activity map. She buys a delta. So the question we should be able to answer, plainly, is not "does the sovereign PMO tool have these features," it is:

Why does this model close the fifty-percent gap that the Ariba-plus-S/4-plus-large-SI shape leaves open?

The answer, one line at a time: because the no-PO-no-pay control is owned from the ledger side, not reconciled quarterly from the vendor side; because the DI centre of excellence measures the hit rate against ACDOCA in real time; because the sovereign agent structure keeps the work list inside a repository the client governs, so supplier alignment stops falling into the crack between the SI and the SAP AE.

3The sovereign PMO, as roles around one table

The traditional discovery-and-planning phase is vendor-led. An SAP account executive fronts it. System integrators sit underneath as channel partners. Nobody in that arrangement is compensated on the outcome; everyone is compensated on the sale. The roll-up that comes out of it is opinion-shaped and depends heavily on who happened to be in the room.

The counter-move is not a different vendor. It is a different authority.

A sovereign team, by role

  • A Finance Director as the sponsor of the case — the person who signs against the number.
  • A CIO as the sovereign of the estate — the person who says which systems are the record.
  • A Data lead — the person who says which datasets are trusted.
  • A Platforms lead — the person who says what the hyperscaler and integration surface actually support.
  • A Strategic Projects lead — the person carrying the MVPs the programme has to demonstrate.
  • Around them, a Steering Committee that meets both the management opinion and the source data at the same table.

The PMO's job, inside that room, is to look both ways. Up, at the management opinion of what the programme should deliver. Down, at whether the underlying data agrees. Business benefit is only real when the two agree, and the PMO is the surface on which they are made to agree.

Practically, that means:

  • Use structured repositories to ingest every existing material — decks, SOWs, SI deliverables, vendor pitches — alongside existing sovereign source data.
  • Build a Decision Intelligence PMO dashboard off that ingested base.
  • Baseline every project artifact against chosen standards. Not whatever standards the previous SI happened to leave behind — standards the sovereign team picked and can defend.
Shift from vendor-led discovery to client-led. — the one-line thesis

Trust boundary for AI, plainly stated

Inside this model, AI trust is limited to data processing of large datasets. The AI is not the strategist and not the decider. It is the instrument that makes the sovereign-source lookback tractable at enterprise scale. Every workstream that touches an operational system arrives with a stated what for. Loading intelligence onto an operational system without that "what for" is the vendor-led move this model is meant to replace.

4Decision Intelligence — the framework that centralises discovery

Inside the sovereign estate, the word AI is not the label. It is the synonym.

Decision Intelligence Framework — synonym: AI.

That reframing matters. It moves the conversation off the vendor's vocabulary and onto a vocabulary the boardroom can govern. AI, as a boardroom term, has been trained by three years of pitch decks. Decision Intelligence is what the discipline actually is inside a sovereign estate: a framework for balancing management opinion against source data, and returning prioritised work to the systems that already run the business.

The framework runs in four moves, in order:

1 · Read the source, in place

Analyse sovereign source data wherever it lives today — through standard data extractors or an existing data lake. The framework does not require a migration to work. That is the sovereignty stance made practical: it reads through the egress the estate already has.

2 · Ingest the vendor materials, into the client's index

Every third-party vendor material — every deck, every SOW, every SI artifact — is pulled into an AI-efficient, indexed, structured repository the client owns. The vendor materials become evidence in the client's index, not orphans on the vendor's SharePoint. This is the direct counter-move to the opinion-shaped roll-up.

3 · Quantify on two clocks at once

Identify quantitative economic benefits. Prioritise technical leakage on the near clock — the rented estate's fractured PTP is where the money is being lost this quarter. Simultaneously consider the future-state primary technical barriers of the entire estate on the longer clock. Two clocks, held without collapsing one into the other.

4 · Feed work lists back into the operational systems

Identify early value-capture targets and begin feeding Decision Intelligence work lists into the operational systems the client already runs. The framework does not sit in a slide. It writes back. This is where the AI trust boundary becomes concrete: the AI produces the prioritised work; humans in the existing systems execute it.

Read together, that is what "centralising discovery under the sovereign" means in practice. Not a new tower next to SAP and the SI — a layer that pulls the vendor materials, the source data, and the work lists under one authority, and that authority is the company, not an external party.

5What I would want us to agree on before the next conversation

These are the framings I want us to share as a starting point. If any of them are wrong for you, that is the more useful conversation than the plan.

  1. That a domain can be named sovereign or rented, and that the PMO's job is to know which is which.
  2. That the outcome delta a CIO buys is not the activity map, it is the closing of the fifty-percent gap the familiar shape leaves open.
  3. That the PMO looks both ways — management opinion up, source data down — and baselines both against chosen standards.
  4. That AI is a synonym for the Decision Intelligence framework, and its trust boundary is data processing of large datasets, not strategy and not decision.
  5. That discovery is client-led, and that the vendor and the SI are evidence sources for the client's index, not the authority above it.

6How the framing maps to New Foundations, role by role

Because Richemont is staffing three roles simultaneously, the framing is only useful if it says something specific about each one. One paragraph each — as a starting point for the walk-through, not as a job description.

S/4HANA Transformation Technical Manager

This is the role that owns the ledger spine. In the sovereign reading, its remit is not to deliver S/4HANA as a product — it is to make ACDOCA legible under the client's authority, via a datasphere semantic layer the client controls. The near-clock question this role answers is: where is technical leakage inside the current ECC estate, and how do we quantify it against ACDOCA in real time rather than reconcile it quarterly? The long-clock question is: what are the primary technical barriers of the future state, held honestly, so the programme does not sell certainty it has not earned?

Scope & Simplification Manager

This is the role that owns the rented-versus-sovereign boundary. Simplification, in a multi-maison estate with fractured PTP and many SAPs, is not deletion — it is naming. The remit is to say, system by system, which side of the boundary it sits on, and to defend the answer against the additive reflex. In the sovereign reading, this is where chosen standards replace existing standards: the scope authority is the person who tells the programme what it will not do, and why the omission is a gain rather than a loss.

Programme Manager

This is the role that owns the looking-both-ways discipline. It sits above the technical and scope authorities and holds the PMO's obligation to balance management opinion against source data. In the sovereign reading, its instrument is the Decision Intelligence PMO dashboard: structured repositories on one side, sovereign source data on the other, baselined against chosen standards. Its interview question — the one it should not be able to dodge — is the one from page two: why does this programme close the fifty-percent gap that the familiar shape leaves open?

Held together, the three roles are the ring around the same table page three names by function — a Finance Director sponsoring the case, a CIO governing the estate, a Data lead vouching for the source, a Platforms lead vouching for the surface, a Strategic Projects lead carrying the MVPs. The New Foundations posting is the programme's own name for the sovereign PMO. The recap is only useful if it helps that name mean the same thing to us as it means to the people already inside it.
